Oil-to-gas ratio

WTI crude divided by Henry Hub natural gas, with the mean-reversion question tested rather than assumed.

They are neither reliably inverse nor reliably direct. Both are energy, so a broad demand shock moves them together — but past that they decouple. Crude is global and transportable; gas is regional and expensive to ship, so US, European and Asian gas can diverge for months. Gas demand is strongly seasonal, crude far less so. And since shale, US gas supply follows its own dynamics rather than oil's. The measured correlation below will show you exactly how unstable this is year to year.

The ratio is the tradeable object, not the direction. Energy equivalence is roughly 6:1 by heat content, but the price ratio has ranged from about 5 to over 60. It mean-reverts — slowly, and with excursions that will take you out before it does. That's what the backtest below measures.
